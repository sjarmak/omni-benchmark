\section{Registered successor condition C5}
\label{sec:c5}

Section~\ref{sec:results} reports a governed condition that never composed a
query through the semantic layer, and Section~\ref{sec:development} explains why:
a compiler that declined to guess published a model with no joins and no
measures, so no compiled path existed for the planner to take. That leaves the
motivating question open rather than answered. C5 is the registered condition
that closes it. It was registered on 2026-08-30, after the held-out aggregates
were opened; its design provenance is the mechanism measurements and the
product's public documentation, not any held-out outcome. That ordering is why it
is confined to the development partition and can never become a held-out
candidate. Its development-partition readout is reported below.

\subsection{What C5 changes}
C4 measured the product under a deployment shape its documentation never
recommends. C5 measures it under the shape the documentation prescribes, built by
a separate compiler entry point from the same public schema, public column
meanings, and public HKB, and nothing else. Six changes define the condition:

\begin{itemize}
\item A view for every public table, 47 to 63 per database, against the
      mechanical baseline's 6 to 11, with a physical dimension for every column
      carrying its public column meaning as description.
\item A join for every foreign-key contract that passes the same conservative
      cardinality rule the relationship experiment used, 1,049 of 1,228 public
      foreign keys. The endpoint filters that bounded that experiment relax on
      their own, because both endpoints are now published.
\item The complete HKB carried into \texttt{ai\_context} rather than pruned: one
      exact field target becomes field-level context, a table-scoped or
      cross-table definition becomes topic-level context with its dependency
      chain inlined prerequisite-first, and database-global knowledge becomes
      model-level context. Formulas are carried verbatim. The compiler enforces
      the product's documented budget, warning at 150{,}000 characters of model
      context per database and failing above 175{,}000.
\item Field metadata only through channels the deployment API's exact-readback
      contract confirms, which excludes \texttt{synonyms} and
      \texttt{sample\_values}.
\item Generic query-pattern guidance at model level, describing the structurally
      fragile shapes from the failure taxonomy once each, instantiated from
      schema structure alone and from no question content.
\item No measures. Measures with grain resolution are registered as a second
      phase and built only if the first phase warrants the spend.
\end{itemize}

Everything that defends the number is unchanged: public-only content provenance,
both frozen scorers reported without post-result selection, one generation,
append-only records, isolated \texttt{livesqlbench-*} branches, per-attempt exact
readback of every deployed document including the model-level context, and the
rule that a wrong answer is never a reason to rerun.

\subsection{Deployment}
The arm required eight deployment passes. Its failures are preserved
with the same discipline as the rest of this record, because each one is a
property of writing a semantic model to this product rather than an incident to
be tidied away.

The first pass failed on 21 views whose CamelCase table names the product renames
on creation. The second reached 8 of 16 databases before the process that owned
it exited, leaving a half-deployed arm; the fix ties the remote identity to the
deployment run's revision, so a retry can never land on a populated branch, and
long jobs now run detached. The third verified 15 of 16 databases with zero
validation issues and exact readback, and failed on
\texttt{planets\_data\_large} at the readback stage after 7 observations. A
read-only comparison of that branch against the committed bundle showed 58 of its
118 documents differing, every view and no topic, carrying the product's own
schema-generated view content rather than ours: the product regenerated the view
layer from the physical schema after the upload landed, on the database with the
largest view surface in the set. The fix uploads a second time when a branch does
not converge and fails only if the reuploaded branch still differs. The fourth
pass carried that fix and reproduced the same single-database readback failure,
which makes the behavior deterministic rather than a timing race. The eighth
pass verified all 16 target databases with zero validation issues and exact
readback, and that deployment is the one the generation ran against.

None of these fixes contains a database name, a question, or a label, and none
can mask a real mismatch, because a branch that still differs after the second
upload fails exactly as before.

\subsection{The C5 result}
The single authorized generation, run \texttt{c5-dev-a-v4}, completed all 136
attempts with no record-write failures: 110 answered and 26 errored. All 26
non-answered attempts carry \texttt{benchmark\_infrastructure} origin and were
put through the exact-readback recovery path; all 26 classified as evaluated-system
failures and none was recovered, so each counts against C5.

\paragraph{Findings.}
On the identical 136-attempt governed frame, C5 scores 18 of 136 (13.2\%) under
the official-compatible scorer against the frozen C4 baseline's 9 of 136
(6.6\%), and 16 of 135 (11.9\%) against 9 of 135 (6.7\%) under the corrected
scorer. On the matched 122-question frame, C5 reaches 10.7\% against C4's 4.1\%
and C2's 23.8\%, closing roughly 45\% of the distance between the mechanical
governed baseline and the direct agent carrying the same knowledge as prose. C5
is the first governed condition in this study to exceed the raw-schema floor C1;
the mechanical baseline C4 fell below it. The gain also comes at lower cost:
across the 136 matched attempts, median total tokens fall from 583{,}188 to
396{,}884, median tool calls from 7 to 3, median database queries from 2 to 1,
and median latency from 50{,}557\,ms to 32{,}492\,ms.

\paragraph{Mechanism.}
Declared joins did not move governed queries off the rewrite path. All 134
parseable captured C5 queries carry \texttt{rewriteSql} with agent-authored SQL,
and none declares a join through the semantic model, despite a published view
for every table and a join for every qualifying foreign key. The same analyzer
applied to six governed arms, three sealed C4 repetitions, the C4 and E02
development arms, and C5, finds 661 of 661 parseable attempts on the rewrite
path and zero composed. The residual rewrite is therefore not explained by the
absence of measures in this phase: no query attempted a compiled path at all,
so no query was blocked by a missing measure.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
The C5 evidence narrows the first of the three statements in
Section~\ref{sec:results} and leaves the other two standing. C4's governed
result was a property of the sparse compiled model, not of the semantic layer
as such: rebuilding the same product to its own documented shape doubles
accuracy on the identical frame. But the gain arrives entirely through the
model acting as context for hand-authored SQL, because composition never
occurred, so the study still contains no measurement of governed semantic
composition at any deployment density. The knowledge-to-semantics compilation
loss, 193 of 1{,}090 definitions with 46.9\% deferred on unresolved grain,
remains the binding constraint on the remaining gap to C2.

\subsection{What C5 will not establish}
Four limits are fixed in advance. C5 is a development-partition condition, and
the held-out frame is closed, so no held-out claim will be made for it whatever it
shows. It postdates visible held-out aggregates, which is why it may not be
promoted into a held-out candidate. It runs a single generation, so a small
difference against a comparator will not be separable from repetition noise the
way the three-repetition held-out arm allows. And phase 1 publishes no measures,
so a null result would not distinguish declared joins failing to help from a
model still missing the aggregation contracts that
Section~\ref{sec:product} identifies as the deeper gap.
