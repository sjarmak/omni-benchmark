\section{Standing of the record, and what is still open}
\label{sec:status}

\paragraph{What is closed.}
The four-condition held-out comparison is complete and terminal. All 1,068
generations executed under Freeze B, both frozen scorers ran in one atomic pass,
and the aggregates in Section~\ref{sec:results} are the result. Nothing in this
study may reopen them: the no-rerun rule forbids regenerating a trial because its
answer was wrong, the metered checkpoint partition is unconsumed and stays that
way, and the visible held-out aggregates may not drive a further intervention or
a promotion. The development record is likewise closed, with E01, E02, and E05
resolved inconclusive by their own stopping rules and E03 and E04 never run.

\paragraph{What is still under human control.}
Per-question held-out labels remain in custody. Only identity-free aggregates
have been released, which is what allows this paper to report accuracy without
reporting which questions were answered. The prespecified post-hoc analyses that
need those labels, the gold-SQL template-overlap audit and the blinded
gold-quality adjudication, therefore remain specified and unexecuted, behind a
human-controlled release. Their absence is a gap in the analysis, not an ambiguity
in the result: neither could change the primary endpoints, both were declared as
separate sensitivity analyses, and the original official score stands regardless.

\paragraph{What the failure taxonomy now contains.}
The protocol version of this paper listed three failure hypotheses and marked
every taxonomy cell unmeasured. Two of the three are now measured, and both were
partly right for the wrong reason. HKB dependency composition was predicted to
lose meaning during transformation; what the record shows is a compiler that
declined to represent 46.9\% of definitions rather than one that represented them
badly, and the loss codes name grain, cardinality, and aggregation as the missing
contracts. Semantic discoverability was predicted to hide correct definitions
from the retrieval surface; what the record shows is that the product did reach
compiled dimensions, on 109 of 135 governed development attempts, and then wrote
its own SQL around them. The third hypothesis, compilation and validation
fidelity, is untested in the sense it was posed, because no semantic compilation
occurred on any governed attempt.

\paragraph{The successor condition.}
One successor condition is reported on development data only.
Section~\ref{sec:c5} defines C5 in full: a governed condition built the way the
product's own documentation prescribes, from the same public inputs under the same
custody rules, confined to the development partition. Its comparisons, its frames,
and its stopping rules were fixed in advance. Eight deployment passes are
preserved with their failures; the last verified all 16 databases with exact
readback. On the identical 136-attempt frame C5 scores 13.2\% against the frozen
governed baseline's 6.6\%, at roughly two-thirds the median token cost, and all
134 of its parseable captured queries still took the product's raw-SQL rewrite
path with no declared join.

The reason to register it in this record rather than to leave it for a later one
is that it addresses a limitation this study cannot repair. The held-out frame is
closed, C5 postdates visible held-out aggregates, and no held-out claim will be
made for it. What it can settle is whether the governed rung fails as a design or
failed here as a deployment, which is the question Section~\ref{sec:results}
leaves open and the one a reader of this paper is most likely to ask next.
